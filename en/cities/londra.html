METROMIND / NETWORK ARCHIVE

How the network took shape

  1. 1863

    The Metropolitan beneath the streets

    In 1863 the Metropolitan Railway opened between Paddington, then Bishop’s Road, and Farringdon Street. Steam trains ran on the original route: the Underground name predates the deep tunnels we now associate with the Tube.

  2. 1870

    Crossing the Thames

    In 1870 the Tower Subway crossed the river through a tunnel excavated with a shield. It was an engineering milestone rather than a playable line in the Underground network.

  3. 1890

    The electric Tube

    In 1890 the City & South London Railway opened a deep electric railway from Stockwell to King William Street. Much of that route is associated with the Northern line. Find Stockwell in the game, but do not look for the old King William Street terminus: the map represents a later network.

  4. 1906–07

    Three new Tube railways beneath central London

    The railways that became the Bakerloo, Piccadilly and the Northern’s Charing Cross branch opened. Deep tunnels became a network of alternatives: at Piccadilly Circus, for example, you can transfer between Bakerloo and Piccadilly.

  5. 1933

    The map changes how passengers navigate

    Harry Beck’s diagram was published. Downplaying geographic distance helps show station order and transfers, the same principle that makes a schematic useful in MetroMind.

  6. 1968–69

    The Victoria line enters service

    The Victoria line introduced automatically controlled trains and new connections through the centre. Find Oxford Circus in the game to see how it intersects older lines.

  7. 1979

    The Jubilee line opens

    The Jubilee line joined the network, initially reaching Charing Cross. That terminus is absent from the Jubilee line in the game: the route changed with the 1999 extension.

  8. 1999

    Jubilee takes a new route east

    From Green Park, the new route passed through Westminster, crossed the Thames towards Waterloo and continued to the Docklands and Stratford. Charing Cross left the Jubilee line, showing how an extension can replace a section as well as add stations.

  9. 2021

    A new branch towards Battersea

    Nine Elms and Battersea Power Station opened on the Northern line. A new branch leaves Kennington: in the game, check which direction you take when your destination is south of the river.

Sources and archives: TfL / London Transport Museum – Tube 160 · TfL – Key dates in London transport · TfL – Northern Line Extension

How to think about this network

This model contains London Underground lines. DLR, the Elizabeth line, Overground and other railway services are excluded. Check that a connection exists in the game diagram before relying on a shortcut.

Branches make the chosen direction’s terminus important. Routes sharing a colour can diverge: study the branch containing your required stop before confirming an automatic ride.

Marked walking connections cost 5 minutes. They can make a nearby pair of stations useful, but visual proximity alone does not create a playable connection: use only the links shown on the map.
