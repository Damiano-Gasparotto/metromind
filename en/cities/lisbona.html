How the network took shape

  1. 1959

    A Y-shaped beginning

    Construction began in 1955 and the metro opened in December 1959. Branches from Sete Rios and Entre Campos joined at Rotunda and shared tracks to Restauradores. The original design already had a fork.

  2. 1963–72

    From the centre to Alvalade

    The network reached Rossio, then Anjos and finally Alvalade. You can recognise that corridor on the Green line in the game: it was built in stages.

  3. 1988

    Extensions resume

    After sixteen years without new openings, tracks reached Colégio Militar/Luz from Sete Rios and Cidade Universitária from Entre Campos. These extensions grew two different corridors.

  4. 1993

    Campo Grande links two corridors

    Sections from Cidade Universitária and Alvalade reached Campo Grande, the first elevated station. In the game, Yellow and Green meet there.

  5. 1995

    A junction becomes an interchange

    In 1995 the branches were separated at Rotunda, now Marquês de Pombal, allowing them to operate as independent lines. On the game map you can still recognise this point as the interchange between Blue and Yellow.

  6. 1998

    A line to the Expo

    In 1998 the Red line connected Alameda to Oriente and served the Expo site. Find Alameda on the map: Red was a separate route rather than another branch of the original trunk, and you can transfer there to Green.

  7. 2012

    Red reaches the airport

    The line continued from Oriente through Moscavide and Encarnação to Aeroporto. In the game, an airport stop takes you along Red beyond its former terminus.

Sources and archives: Metropolitano de Lisboa

How to think about this network

Azul, Amarela, Verde and Vermelha identify the game’s lines. Group required stops sharing a line before boarding: you may be able to visit them during one ride.

Find Baixa-Chiado, Alameda, São Sebastião and Saldanha on the map to orient yourself between lines. Look at which pairs meet: an interchange near the destination can be less useful than one near a required stop.

With one required stop, compare visiting it before transferring towards the finish with transferring early and approaching it from the other side. The geometric distance on the diagram does not replace counting stops.
