How the network took shape

  1. 1964

    The first Red line

    On 1 November 1964, M1 opened from Lotto to Sesto Marelli. Those were the two termini of the original cross-city route; the endpoints you see on today’s map are the result of later extensions.

  2. 1964

    Making the network readable

    The first line came with a coordinated design for finding your way underground. Franco Albini and Franca Helg worked on the station interiors and Bob Noorda on the graphics. Line colour became a cue passengers could follow along their journey.

  3. 1969

    Green adds a second route

    M2 opened between Caiazzo and Cascina Gobba. Green meets Red at Loreto: in the game, try what changes when a station offers two lines to continue on.

  4. 1990

    Yellow runs from Centrale to Duomo

    M3 entered service between Centrale and Duomo. The first section connected two hubs: Green meets it at Centrale and Red at Duomo. On the map, try using Yellow to travel between them.

  5. 2013

    Lilac opens a new generation

    The automatic M5 opened in 2013. Its extension towards San Siro added a westward connection. In the game, reach it from Red at Lotto or from Green at Garibaldi FS.

  6. 2022

    Blue opens in the east

    M4 entered service with its first section on the Linate side. Opening in stages explains why the start date differs from the year the line could be ridden from end to end.

  7. 2023

    Blue meets Red

    Tricolore and San Babila opened on 4 July. Blue reached the centre and connected to M1, making it possible to reach Linate by changing from the Red line.

  8. 2024

    The Blue line crosses Milan

    On 12 October 2024, the full M4 route from San Cristoforo to Linate entered service. In the game, find San Babila, where this new cross-city route meets the first line. Consider how that transfer changes a journey to the airport.

How to think about this network

On M1 and M2, the destination of the train matters. Trains sharing a line colour can take different branches: trace the branch containing your required stop before choosing a direction.

Duomo, Cadorna FN and Centrale FS help you orient yourself in the centre of the network. A route that looks longer can be cheaper when it avoids a transfer: changing lines costs as much as travelling two stops.

The model includes a walking connection between Missori and Sforza Policlinico. Use it when the saving over your full route outweighs its 5-minute cost.

Interchanges to know

These stations connect multiple lines in our model. Study them before starting a mission; having many connections does not automatically make a station the best choice.
