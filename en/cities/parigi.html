METROMIND / NETWORK ARCHIVE

How the network took shape

  1. 1898

    From decision to construction

    In 1898 a law declared the project a public utility. The city built the infrastructure under Fulgence Bienvenüe and entrusted operations to the CMP. The metro was a response to congestion on the streets.

  2. 1900

    The first crossing

    Line 1 opened between Porte Maillot and Porte de Vincennes on 19 July 1900, during the Universal Exhibition. Both stations are still on line 1 in the game, even though the route now extends beyond them.

  3. 1908–10

    Line 4 goes beneath the Seine

    Construction proceeded from both banks. Ground freezing helped deal with unstable soil. In 1910 the central section joined the two halves: find Cité on line 4 to locate that crossing in the game.

  4. 1911

    A second company builds north

    The Nord-Sud company opened line B between Saint-Lazare and Porte de Saint-Ouen. That section became part of line 13, a much longer route in the network we know today.

  5. 1934

    Line 1 moves beyond Porte de Vincennes

    Extending the line to Château de Vincennes made the old reversing loop at the terminus redundant. Infrastructure built for turning trains back lost its purpose once the line continued beyond it.

  6. 1956

    Rubber-tyred trains enter service

    Line 11 was the first in the network to use them in service; line 1 followed in 1964. In MetroMind, game rules determine travel time, so tyres and steel wheels do not change the cost of a move.

  7. 1998

    A second rapid route

    Line 14 entered service in 1998, designed for automatic operation. Compare it with line 1 in the game: for a required stop near a shared interchange, a route with fewer stops may offset the cost of transferring.

  8. 2013–22

    Line 4 extends beyond its old limits

    After running between Porte d’Orléans and Porte de Clignancourt, line 4 extended first to Montrouge and then to Bagneux. The changing terminus shows how a historic corridor can continue growing.

  9. 2024

    Line 14 connects new destinations

    Extensions to Saint-Denis–Pleyel in the north and Orly in the south took line 14 beyond the scale of its original project. The new stations expanded the range of journeys that could cross Paris without changing lines.

How to think about this network

The game uses Métro lines; RER, trams and regional trains are excluded. 3bis and 7bis are distinct lines and should be treated separately when planning.

A dense network makes it easy to transfer too often. An intersection does not mean you should get off: compare a longer direct ride with two shorter rides and include the 4-minute transfer penalty.

With two or three required stops, try a different visiting order. A stop that looks close to the start can be cheaper to visit later when it shares a line with the destination.

Interchanges to know

These stations connect multiple lines in our model. Study them before starting a mission; having many connections does not automatically make a station the best choice.
