How the network took shape

  1. 1902

    A railway above the streets too

    Berlin’s first electric network opened in 1902. The original route combined elevated and underground sections: U-Bahn does not mean that every track runs below ground.

  2. 1902

    Reading the viaduct

    The historic elevated railway crosses Kreuzberg towards Nollendorfplatz. You can recognise part of that corridor on the game’s earliest lines. Compare the lines serving an interchange before choosing which one to follow.

  3. 1923

    The first wide-profile line

    The oldest section of what became U6 opened as line C. Its trains used a larger profile than the earliest lines. The difference reflects two generations of construction within the same U-Bahn.

  4. 1961

    A divided network

    When the Wall went up in 1961, the division broke connections and closed some stations even where trains still passed through. The game models a contemporary network and does not reproduce those historical interruptions.

  5. 1989

    Connections begin to reopen

    After the Wall fell, trains began stopping at stations closed during the division. Even before new tunnels could be built, reopening access changed which journeys were possible.

How to think about this network

This diagram contains U-Bahn lines U1 to U9. S-Bahn, trams and buses are excluded. It is a game model and does not simulate engineering works, replacement services or operational disruptions.

Shared sections can show multiple colours between the same stops. Choosing a line compatible with your onward journey avoids later transfers: the line on which you arrive matters to the time calculation.

Connections around Nollendorfplatz, Wittenbergplatz and Alexanderplatz offer several options. Compare the full journey with all remaining required stops instead of using a hub simply because it has many connections.
