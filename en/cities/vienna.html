How the network took shape

  1. 1976

    Reusing the Stadtbahn

    In 1976, U4 trials ran between Heiligenstadt and Friedensbrücke on a section of the former Stadtbahn. Vienna’s metro grew partly by adapting existing railway corridors.

  2. 1978

    Building a new route

    In February 1978 the first purpose-built U-Bahn section opened on U1 between Karlsplatz and Reumannplatz. Building a new line and adapting an older railway were two different ways to add connections to the same network.

  3. 1980

    U2 arrives

    U2 opened between Karlsplatz and Schottenring. The core network took shape as another corridor met the other lines at important passenger hubs.

  4. 1982

    A network of transfers

    By 1982 the core U1, U2 and U4 network was complete. Find Karlsplatz in the game to see how a new route fits into the network. With several lines available, choose the one that helps you reach your next required stop.

  5. 1989

    U6 brings in another corridor

    U6 brought another stretch of the old Stadtbahn into the metro, along the Gürtel. Find Westbahnhof in the game to switch between the U6 corridor and U3.

  6. 1991

    U3 adds a crossing

    The opening of U3 added an east–west route. Find Stephansplatz in the game, where U3 meets U1 in the city centre.

Sources and archives: Stadt Wien – Die U4 · Wiener Linien

How to think about this network

The line numbers are not consecutive: this game contains five lines and does not include U5. Use the line list below to understand the exact model you are studying.

Karlsplatz, Stephansplatz and Westbahnhof help you get familiar with the interchanges. A central hub is not automatically a shortcut: count the transfers and stops needed to reach it.

Required stops on U6 can need a different approach from central stops. Find where its line connects with the destination’s line, then check whether you can collect another required stop on the way.
